South West Region (Manyu) • Bayangi Heritage

🌿 Eru (Wild Spinach & Waterleaf Stew)

A legendary forest delicacy marrying dark, fibrous shredded climbing vines (Gnetum africanum) with tender, succulent waterleaf, slow-cooked beef, gelatinous canda, smoked fish, and a golden red palm oil emulsion.

Difficulty: Medium
Prep Time: 45 mins
Cook Time: 1h 15m
Primary Pairing: Water Fufu or Garri
6
(Ingredients automatically scale!)

🛒 Ingredients (Interactive Checklist)

👨‍🍳 Step-by-Step Cooking Guide

  1. Tenderize the Meats: Simmer diced beef and canda with 1 bouillon cube, salt, and 300ml water for 40 minutes until tender and liquid is reduced.
  2. Add Smoked Fish & Waterleaf: Fold in flaked smoked fish and half the crayfish. Layer chopped waterleaf on top. Cover without adding water—the leaves release plenty of natural moisture.
  3. Incorporate Eru Leaves: Add the shredded Eru over the wilting waterleaf. Simmer uncovered on medium-low for 10 minutes until Eru softens.
  4. The Golden Palm Oil Finish: Pour red palm oil generously over the greens. Add remaining crayfish and scotch bonnet. Simmer on low heat for 15 minutes until excess moisture evaporates and glossy oil crowns the edges.
💡 Chef's Golden Rule

Always maintain a 2:1 ratio of waterleaf to Eru. The abundant moisture of the waterleaf tenderizes the wild forest leaves so you never end up with dry or crunchy Eru.

South West & Littoral • Bakweri & Bafaw Tradition

🍠 Ekwang (Grated Cocoyam Parcels in Palm Oil Gravy)

A communal masterpiece of freshly grated red cocoyams wrapped by hand into tender leaves, then slow-simmered in an intoxicating bath of country onions, toasted pèbè (wild nutmeg), smoked seafood, and virgin palm oil.

Difficulty: Advanced
Prep Time: 1h 30m
Cook Time: 1h 00m
Tradition: Never stir with a spoon!
6
(Ingredients automatically scale!)

🛒 Ingredients (Interactive Checklist)

👨‍🍳 Step-by-Step Cooking Guide

  1. Grate & Season Cocoyam: Finely grate the cocoyam until viscous. Season with 1 tsp salt. Blanch leaf wrappers in warm water for 2 minutes to make pliable.
  2. Roll the Parcels: Place a leaf flat, add a tablespoon of cocoyam paste, and roll tightly into a slender finger-shaped cylinder.
  3. Layer the Pot: Oil the bottom of a heavy pot, add canda and fish bones to shield the base, then pack rolls tightly in concentric circles with crayfish and smoked fish between layers.
  4. Simmer Without Stirring: Whisk warm stock with ground country onion, pèbè, and pepper. Pour gently down the sides of the pot. Drizzle palm oil over the top. Simmer covered on low heat for 50-60 minutes.
⚠️ Cardinal Rule

NEVER STIR EKWANG WITH A SPOON! Stirring crushes the parcels into a gummy mash. Only gently swirl or shake the pot by its handles every 15 minutes.

West & Littoral • Bamiléké & Bassa Heritage

🫘 Koki (Steamed Golden Black-Eyed Pea Pudding)

A magnificent golden-orange savory steamed pudding. Peeled black-eyed cowpeas are beaten to incorporate airy lightness, blended with warm red palm oil into a vibrant emulsion, wrapped in charred banana leaves and slow-steamed.

Difficulty: Medium
Prep Time: 1 hour
Steam Time: 2h 30m
Pairing: Steamed Ripe Plantains or Bobolo
6
(Ingredients automatically scale!)

🛒 Ingredients (Interactive Checklist)

👨‍🍳 Step-by-Step Cooking Guide

  1. De-hull the Peas: Soak peas for 25 minutes. Rub between palms to loosen skins, rinse thoroughly until only pristine white beans remain.
  2. Whip to a Light Mousse: Puree white beans with warm water until velvety. Whisk vigorously for 8-10 minutes to incorporate air for a fluffy crumb.
  3. Warm Palm Oil Emulsion: Pour warm palm oil slowly into whipped batter while stirring. The white batter instantly turns dazzling sunset-orange. Dissolve salt and fold in.
  4. Wrap & Long-Steam: Ladle 1.5 cups batter into double-layered softened banana leaves with a scotch bonnet. Tie tightly and steam for 2 to 2.5 hours on a rack over boiling water.
💡 Chef's Secret

Always use lukewarm water when blending. Cold water causes the warm palm oil to congeal into specks, preventing the smooth golden emulsion.

Centre & South Regions • Beti & Ewondo Tradition

🍃 O'kok (Finely Sliced Forest Leaves with Peanuts & Palm Oil)

An extraordinary sweet-and-savory masterpiece of Central Cameroon. Micro-shredded wild Okok leaves simmered slowly in rich roasted peanut butter, fragrant palm nut pulp, with a delicate balance of cane sugar and sea salt.

Difficulty: Medium
Prep Time: 40 mins
Cook Time: 1h 15m
Signature Pair: Bobolo or Miondo (Fermented Cassava)
6
(Ingredients automatically scale!)

🛒 Ingredients (Interactive Checklist)

👨‍🍳 Step-by-Step Cooking Guide

  1. Roast & Puree Peanuts: Roast raw groundnuts until fragrant and golden. Rub off skins, then blend with 200ml warm water into smooth peanut butter.
  2. Parboil Okok Ribbons: Boil shredded Okok leaves with 500ml water and 1/2 tsp salt for 25 minutes until tender and water is absorbed.
  3. Add Palm Nut Pulp / Oil: Pour palm pulp over the leaves and simmer for 15 minutes over medium-low heat.
  4. The Nutty Sweet Simmer: Stir in roasted peanut paste, sugar, and salt. Simmer gently on low heat for 20-25 minutes, stirring continuously from the bottom so the peanut sauce develops its signature sweet-savory amber glaze.
💡 Perfect Harmony

The sour tang of fermented cassava batons (Bobolo / Miondo) cuts through the rich, sweet peanut and palm oil sauce in a pairing made in culinary heaven.

Littoral Region • Sawa & Duala Heritage • National Dish

👑 Ndolé (Cameroon's National Dish: Bitterleaf, Peanut & Royal Prawns)

The undisputed crowning glory of Cameroonian cuisine. Washed bitterleaves simmered in a velvety white puree of peeled raw peanuts, garlic, and ginger with tender meats and smoked fish, crowned with a dramatic pour of sizzling hot caramelized onions and juicy king prawns.

Difficulty: Advanced
Prep Time: 1 hour
Cook Time: 1h 30m
Supreme Pairing: Fried Ripe Plantains (Misi/Dodo)
8
(Ingredients automatically scale!)

🛒 Ingredients (Interactive Checklist)

👨‍🍳 Step-by-Step Cooking Guide

  1. Velvet Peanut Puree: Boil raw peanuts for 10 minutes to slip off skins. Blend white kernels with onions, garlic, ginger, and 200ml water until silky white.
  2. Simmer Meats & Seafood: Stew beef and canda with seasoning for 35 minutes until fork-tender. Add smoked fish and half the crayfish.
  3. Cook Peanut Cream: Pour the white peanut puree into the simmering meat pot. Stir gently over low heat for 25 minutes until rich peanut oils rise.
  4. Fold in Bitterleaves: Loosen washed bitterleaf balls, fold into peanut cream with remaining crayfish, and simmer for 15 minutes.
  5. The Sizzling Garniture: Sauté red onion rings in hot oil until golden; toss in prawns for 3 minutes until pink. Pour sizzling hot oil, sweet onions, and prawns directly over the Ndolé pot!
👑 Royal Presentation

The dramatic tableside pour of sizzling hot oil, caramelized onions, and pink shrimp creates the signature aroma and irresistible golden crown of authentic Duala Ndolé.
