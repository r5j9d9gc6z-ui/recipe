# 🫘 Koki (Steamed Golden Black-Eyed Pea Pudding)

> **Also known as:** Koki Corn, Koki Beans, Gâteau de Koki  
> **Region of Origin:** West & Littoral Regions, Cameroon (Bamiléké & Bassa cultural heritage)  
> **Category:** Savory Steamed Bean Pudding / Tamale  
> **Difficulty:** Medium  
> **Prep Time:** 1 hour (soaking & peeling beans)  
> **Cook Time:** 2 hours to 2 hours 30 minutes (steaming)  
> **Servings:** 6 to 8 parcels  

---

## 📖 Culinary Story & Cultural Significance

Koki is a majestic, golden-orange steamed cake made from specially prepared cowpeas or black-eyed peas, unrefined virgin red palm oil, water, and chili. A culinary treasure of the Bamiléké people in the lush Western highlands of Cameroon, Koki is a fixture at cultural ceremonies, celebrations, and Sunday family gatherings.

What elevates Koki to culinary art is its deceptive simplicity. With only five core ingredients—beans, palm oil, water, salt, and peppers—its success lies entirely in mastery of technique: thoroughly de-hulling the bean skins, incorporating air into the paste to create a light, soufflé-like crumb, balancing the palm oil emulsion, and wrapping it skillfully inside flame-softened banana leaves before a long, steady steam.

---

## 🛒 Ingredients List

### The Foundation:
- **500g (1.1 lbs) Dried Black-Eyed Peas or White Koki Cowpeas**
- **300ml to 400ml Lukewarm water** (for blending and emulsifying)

### The Emulsion & Aromatics:
- **250ml (1 cup) Fresh unrefined Red Palm Oil** (gently warmed until completely liquefied)
- **2 to 3 Whole Scotch Bonnet peppers** (habanero), kept whole or pounded to taste
- **1.5 teaspoons Fine sea salt** (adjust to taste)
- *Optional:* 1 small crushed bouillon cube (optional; purists prefer only salt to highlight the sweet bean flavor)
- *Optional traditional touch:* Tender cocoyam leaves or spinach ribbons tucked inside each packet

### Wrapping & Steaming Materials:
- **Fresh Banana or Plantain leaves** (or aluminum foil lined with parchment paper as a modern substitute)
- **Kitchen cooking twine** or stripped plantain leaf fibers for tying
- A large steamer pot or a heavy stockpot fitted with a steaming rack or base cushion of leaf stems

---

## 👨‍🍳 Step-by-Step Cooking Instructions

### Step 1: Soak and De-hull the Peas
1. Place the dried black-eyed peas in a large bowl and cover with cold water for 25 to 30 minutes (do not over-soak, or the skins become gummy and hard to peel).
2. Rub handfuls of beans vigorously between your palms or pulse lightly in a blender with water for 5 seconds to crack the hulls loose from the beans.
3. Swirl with water; the light husks will float to the top. Pour off the water and husks through a strainer. Repeat 4 to 5 times until the beans are pristine and completely white.

### Step 2: Grind to a Fluffy Mousse
1. Transfer the de-hulled white beans to a high-powered blender or food mill.
2. Add warm water in stages (about 250ml initially) and blend until completely smooth and velvety. The paste should not feel gritty between your fingers.
3. Pour the puree into a large, wide mixing bowl or traditional wooden mortar.
4. Using a sturdy wooden spoon or whisk, beat the mixture vigorously in a circular motion for 8 to 10 minutes. This introduces air pockets, ensuring your finished Koki is tender and fluffy rather than dense.

### Step 3: Emulsify with Warm Palm Oil
1. Gently warm the red palm oil in a small pan until fluid and translucent (do not overheat or fry it).
2. Gradually pour the warm palm oil into the whipped bean batter in a steady stream while continuously stirring.
3. Watch the transformation: the stark white batter will rapidly emulsify into a dazzling, creamy sunset-orange batter.
4. Dissolve 1.5 teaspoons of salt in 50ml of warm water and stir into the batter. Drop in the scotch bonnet peppers. Taste the raw batter—it should taste pleasantly savory with a hint of natural bean sweetness.

### Step 4: Prepare the Banana Leaves
1. Wash banana leaves clean and pat dry.
2. Pass the leaves gently over an open flame on your stovetop for 3 to 5 seconds per side until they soften, turn pliable, and release an intoxicating tea-like botanical aroma.
3. Cut into sheets measuring approximately 30x30 cm.

### Step 5: Wrap the Parcels
1. Double-layer two banana leaf sheets across a deep soup bowl to form a pouch.
2. Ladle 1 to 1.5 cups of golden Koki batter into the center of the leaves.
3. Gather the edges of the banana leaves upward, fold them securely over each other, and bind tightly with kitchen twine or banana fibers to prevent water from seeping inside.

### Step 6: The Long Steam
1. Place a metal steaming rack or a bed of leftover banana leaf stems at the bottom of a wide stockpot. Pour 3 to 4 cm of water underneath the rack.
2. Arrange the wrapped Koki packets upright or flat across the rack.
3. Cover the packets with a final layer of banana leaves, then close the pot with a tight-fitting lid.
4. Steam over medium heat for **2 to 2.5 hours**. Check every 35 minutes and replenish boiling water along the sides of the pot if the water level drops.
5. Once cooked, the packets will feel firm and springy to the touch. Remove from the pot and let stand for 15 minutes before unwrapping.

## 🍽️ Serving Traditions & Pairings

- **Steamed Ripe Plantains:** The natural sweetness of ripe boiled or steamed plantains offers an exquisite counterpoint to the rich, earthy, savory Koki cake.
- **Bobolo & Miondo:** Traditional fermented cassava batons provide a tangy contrast.
- **Boiled Cassava or Sweet Potatoes:** Hearty rustic pairings common in village feasts.

---

## 💡 Chef's Secrets & Pro Tips

1. **The Whipping Step is Key:** Never skip beating the batter before adding the oil. Air incorporation is what gives Koki its soufflé texture!
2. **Warm Water Only:** Always use warm (not cold) water when blending and mixing. Cold water hardens the palm oil into beads and ruins the velvety emulsion.
3. **No Leavening Needed:** Traditional Koki contains no baking powder or eggs—pure bean protein and air do all the lifting.
