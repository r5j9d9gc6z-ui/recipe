# 👑 Ndolé (Cameroon’s National Dish: Bitterleaf, Peanut & Royal Prawn Stew)

> **Also known as:** Le Ndolè National, Royal Ndolé  
> **Region of Origin:** Littoral Region, Cameroon (Duala, Malimba & Sawa heritage)  
> **Category:** Festive Bitterleaf & Groundnut Stew  
> **Difficulty:** Advanced  
> **Prep Time:** 1 hour (peeling peanuts & washing leaves)  
> **Cook Time:** 1 hour 30 minutes  
> **Servings:** 8 to 10 banquet servings  

---

## 📖 Culinary Story & Cultural Significance

Ndolé is universally hailed as the crowned jewel and unofficial national dish of Cameroon. Originating among the coastal Sawa and Duala people along the Wouri River estuary, Ndolé has transcended ethnic boundaries to become the undisputed centerpiece of Cameroonian celebrations, state dinners, weddings, and prestigious banquets.

The dish is a culinary masterclass in balancing contrasts. Bitterleaf (*Vernonia amygdalina*), which undergoes a rigorous washing and rinsing process with food-grade natron (*bilok*) to tame its sharp bitterness into a delicate herbal astringency, is simmered in a velvety, white, unroasted peanut puree perfumed with ginger, garlic, and caramelized onions. Tender beef, gelatinous cow skin (*canda*), and smoked fish infuse the base with profound depth. Finally, just before presenting the dish to the table, the cook performs the crowning ritual: sizzling hot oil laden with crisp caramelized onions and succulent pink tiger prawns is poured over the pot with a theatrical, fragrant sizzle.

## 🛒 Ingredients List

### The Bitterleaf Base:
- **500g (approx. 5 balls) Fresh or Frozen Ndolé / Bitterleaves** (*Vernonia amygdalina*), washed, squeezed, and finely shredded
- **1/2 teaspoon Natron (baking soda / trona)**, used during leaf blanching to preserve vibrant green color and reduce bitterness

### The White Peanut Puree:
- **500g (1.1 lbs) Raw (unroasted) Peanuts (Groundnuts)**, boiled for 10 minutes and peeled white
- **2 medium White or Yellow onions**, roughly chopped
- **5 cloves Fresh garlic**, peeled
- **3 cm piece Fresh ginger**, peeled
- **200ml Water** (for blending)

### Meats & Dried Seafood:
- **600g (1.3 lbs) Stewing beef chuck or goat meat**, cut into bite-sized pieces
- **300g (0.66 lbs) Canda / Kpomo** (cow skin), thoroughly cleaned and boiled until tender
- **200g Smoked fish** (mackerel or stockfish / dry cod), rehydrated and deboned
- **100g (1 cup) Dried crayfish**, ground into coarse meal

### The Royal Sautéed Garniture (Topping):
- **400g (0.9 lbs) Fresh large prawns or tiger shrimp**, peeled and deveined (heads or tails on for presentation)
- **1 large Red onion**, sliced into paper-thin rings
- **100ml Pure sunflower, peanut, or vegetable oil**

### Seasonings:
- **3 Bouillon cubes** (Maggi beef / shrimp)
- **1 teaspoon Salt** (adjust to taste)
- **2 Scotch bonnet peppers**, left whole for perfume without breaking heat

## 👨‍🍳 Step-by-Step Cooking Instructions

### Step 1: Prep and Wash the Bitterleaf
1. If using fresh bitterleaves: boil them in a large pot with 1/2 teaspoon of natron (or baking soda) for 15 minutes. Drain and rinse thoroughly with cold running water, massaging the leaves vigorously with both hands to squeeze out the bitter green juice.
2. Repeat rinsing and squeezing 2 to 3 times until the leaves retain only a faint, pleasing herbal bitterness. Squeeze into tight balls and set aside. (If using store-bought pre-washed frozen ndolé, thaw, rinse once, and squeeze dry).

### Step 2: Prepare the Velvet Peanut Paste
1. Bring raw peanuts to a boil in water for 10 minutes to loosen skins. Drain, cool slightly, and slip off the skins by pinching the peanuts between your fingers until you have clean white kernels.
2. In a blender, combine the white peeled peanuts, chopped yellow onions, garlic cloves, fresh ginger, and 200ml water.
3. Blend continuously on high speed until you obtain an ultra-smooth, creamy white puree.

### Step 3: Simmer the Meats & Broth
1. In a large heavy-bottomed Dutch oven, combine the diced beef and canda. Season with 1 crushed bouillon cube, 1/2 teaspoon salt, and 1 sliced onion.
2. Add 400ml water and simmer on medium heat for 35 to 40 minutes until the meats are succulent and tender.
3. Add the flaked smoked fish/stockfish and half of the ground crayfish. Simmer for another 5 minutes to infuse the savory seafood essence into the broth.

### Step 4: Cook the Peanut Sauce
1. Pour the blended white peanut-aromatic puree into the simmering meat pot.
2. Stir continuously over medium-low heat. As the peanut sauce cooks, it will thicken into a rich, luxurious cream.
3. Drop in the two whole scotch bonnet peppers and remaining bouillon cubes.
4. Cook gently for 20 to 25 minutes, stirring frequently across the bottom of the pot to prevent scorching. You will notice tiny droplets of natural peanut oil rising to the surface.

### Step 5: Incorporate the Ndolé Bitterleaves
1. Fluff and loosen the squeezed balls of washed bitterleaf with your fingers.
2. Fold the bitterleaves into the bubbling peanut sauce.
3. Sprinkle the remaining ground crayfish over the mixture.
4. Stir until the dark green ribbons of bitterleaf are thoroughly suspended throughout the creamy peanut base. Simmer on low heat for 12 to 15 minutes to allow the leaves to absorb the rich flavors.
5. Taste and add salt if needed. Keep the pot warm over low heat.

### Step 6: The Royal Garniture Finale
1. In a separate skillet or frying pan, heat 100ml of vegetable oil over medium-high heat.
2. Add the sliced red onion rings and sauté until translucent, fragrant, and lightly caramelized around the edges (about 4 minutes).
3. Toss in the peeled prawns/shrimp and season with a pinch of salt. Flash-fry for 3 to 4 minutes until the prawns turn vibrant pink, opaque, and succulent.
4. **The Dramatic Finish:** Immediately pour the sizzling hot oil, caramelized onions, and tender pink prawns directly over the surface of the Ndolé pot!
5. Present the pot at the table with the golden prawns and sweet onions glistening on top.

## 🍽️ Serving Traditions & Royal Pairings

- **Fried Ripe Plantains (Misi / Dodo):** The sweet caramelization of golden fried plantains is the supreme, internationally acclaimed partner to Ndolé.
- **Bobolo & Miondo:** Traditional fermented cassava batons provide a chewy, slightly sour accompaniment that complements the rich groundnut sauce.
- **Boiled Plantains or Cocoyams:** A comforting, traditional banquet staple.

## 💡 Chef's Secrets & Pro Tips

1. **Never Skip the Boiling of Peanuts:** Boiling the peanuts before peeling makes removing the skins effortless and eliminates the grassy raw peanut flavor.
2. **The Natron Trick:** Natron preserves the brilliant emerald color of the bitterleaf and breaks down fibrous bitterness quickly without destroying leaf texture.
3. **The Sizzling Oil Pour:** The finishing oil must be sizzling hot when poured over the Ndolé. It locks in the aromatics of the caramelized onions and seals the dish with a showstopping sheen.
