# 🍠 Ekwang (Grated Cocoyam Leaf Rolls in Rich Palm Oil)

> **Also known as:** Ekwan, Coco-leaf Parcels  
> **Region of Origin:** Bakweri, Oroko & Bafaw Peoples (South West & Littoral Regions, Cameroon)  
> **Category:** Traditional Wrapped Root-Vegetable Stew  
> **Difficulty:** Advanced (Requires Patience & Hand Crafting)  
> **Prep Time:** 1 hour 30 minutes (wrapping rolls)  
> **Cook Time:** 1 hour 15 minutes  
> **Servings:** 6 to 8 servings  

---

## 📖 Culinary Story & Cultural Significance

Ekwang is celebrated as one of Central Africa's greatest culinary testaments to patience, communal craftsmanship, and love. Traditionally prepared during grand family feasts, weddings, and festive holidays, wrapping Ekwang is a social activity where mothers, daughters, aunts, and sisters sit together around wooden trays, chatting and meticulously rolling small parcels.

The dish consists of peeled, freshly grated red cocoyams (*taro/macabo*), seasoned simply with salt, wrapped by the teaspoonful into young, tender cocoyam leaves (or bitterleaf/collards). These miniature parcels are neatly packed in concentric circles into a heavy cooking pot, drenched in golden palm oil, smoked fish, succulent canda, and seasoned with a symphony of ancestral Cameroonian forest spices—notably **Country Onions** (*Afrostyrax lepidophyllus*) and aromatic roasted **Pèbè** (African wild nutmeg).

---

## 🛒 Ingredients List

### For the Cocoyam Parcels:
- **1.5 kg (3.3 lbs) Red Cocoyams** (*Macabo Rouge* / Taro), peeled, washed, and finely grated
- **1 kg (2.2 lbs) Young Cocoyam leaves**, collard greens, romaine lettuce, or washed bitterleaves
- **1 teaspoon Salt** (for seasoning the grated cocoyam batter)

### Proteins & Aromatics:
- **400g (0.9 lbs) Canda / Kpomo** (cow skin), boiled until soft and sliced into bite-sized strips
- **300g (0.66 lbs) Smoked catfish or cod**, washed, deboned, and flaked
- **150g (1.5 cups) Dried ground crayfish**
- **1 large Red onion**, finely minced
- **3 to 4 Scotch bonnet peppers**, pounded into a rough paste

### Ancestral Spices & Oils:
- **2 Country onions** (*Afrostyrax lepidophyllus* / Rondeletia bark), ground
- **3 Pèbè seeds** (African wild nutmeg), cracked, toasted, and ground into fine powder
- **300ml (1.25 cups) Pure unrefined Red Palm Oil**
- **3 Bouillon cubes** (Maggi crayfish or beef)
- **800ml to 1 Liter Warm water or concentrated meat stock**

---

## 👨‍🍳 Step-by-Step Cooking Instructions

### Step 1: Prep the Leaves and Cocoyam Batter
1. **Prepare the Grated Cocoyam:** Peel the cocoyams and grate them using the finest side of a box grater or food processor until you achieve a smooth, viscous paste. Stir in 1 teaspoon of salt. Do not add water.
2. **Prepare the Wrappers:** Wash the leaves thoroughly. Cut out the tough fibrous center stems. Tear or slice leaves into palm-sized rectangular sheets (approx. 8x10 cm). If using fresh cocoyam leaves or collards, quickly blanch them in warm water for 2 minutes so they become soft and supple without tearing.

### Step 2: Assemble the Parcels (Communal Crafting)
1. Place a leaf wrapper flat on your palm or a clean cutting board.
2. Place a small tablespoon of grated cocoyam near the bottom edge of the leaf.
3. Roll the leaf tightly around the cocoyam paste to form a slender, finger-shaped cigar. The paste is sticky and will hold the roll closed naturally.
4. Repeat until all cocoyam paste is wrapped.

### Step 3: Layer the Pot
1. Coat the bottom of a heavy-bottomed Dutch oven or pot with 2 tablespoons of red palm oil.
2. Place a layer of fish bones, canda, and smoked fish scraps on the bottom (this creates a protective barrier that keeps the parcels from sticking or scorching).
3. Arrange the rolled Ekwang parcels tightly in neat concentric rings or criss-cross patterns inside the pot.
4. Scatter flakes of smoked fish, canda strips, and half of the ground crayfish between layers.

### Step 4: The Spiced Infusion & The Golden Simmer
1. In a separate bowl, whisk together the warm stock/water, ground country onions, toasted pèbè, minced onions, pounded scotch bonnet peppers, remaining crayfish, and crumbled bouillon cubes.
2. Pour the warm spiced stock gently along the sides of the pot until it reaches just below the top layer of rolls.
3. Pour the red palm oil evenly over the top layer of parcels.
4. Place the pot on medium-high heat. Bring to an energetic boil, then immediately turn heat down to low.

### Step 5: The Cardinal Rule: Never Stir With a Spoon!
1. **CRITICAL:** Do NOT stir Ekwang with a spoon or spatula while cooking! Stirring breaks the delicate parcels and turns the dish into a gummy porridge.
2. Instead, grab the pot by its handles with heat-resistant mitts and give the pot a gentle, steady circular swirl every 15 minutes to circulate the broth and oil.
3. Cover and simmer on low heat for 50 to 60 minutes. The cocoyam inside each parcel will firm up and absorb the aromatic spiced palm oil.
4. When the liquid thickens into a glossy, velvety orange gravy around the firm parcels, remove from heat and let sit uncovered for 10 minutes to set.

## 🍽️ Serving Traditions

- Ekwang is a complete, self-contained one-pot masterpiece: carbohydrates, rich greens, savory proteins, and unctuous sauce are all encapsulated in each bite.
- Spoon gently onto wide platters using a flat ladle to keep each finger-sized parcel intact.
- Garnish with extra whole fresh scotch bonnet peppers for heat enthusiasts.
- Serve warm. It tastes even better the next morning!

---

## 💡 Chef's Secrets & Pro Tips

1. **Choosing Cocoyams:** Use red cocoyam (*Macabo rouge*) if possible; it has the starch density needed to keep the rolls firm rather than soggy.
2. **Preventing Itchiness:** Some wild cocoyams contain calcium oxalate crystals. Always ensure Ekwang cooks for at least 50 minutes to completely neutralize any itchiness.
3. **Resting Time:** Allowing the pot to rest off the flame for 10-15 minutes before serving allows the starch to tighten, giving perfect clean bites.
